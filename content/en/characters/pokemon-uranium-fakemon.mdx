The Pokemon Uranium fakemon are the reason this fan-made RPG still has an active community years after its release. Pokémon Uranium does not simply remix an existing region — it ships an entirely new Pokédex, an entirely new type, and a story built around creatures you have never encountered before. If you are returning to Tandor or hearing about it for the first time, understanding the Pokemon Uranium fakemon roster is the fastest way to understand what makes the game worth your time.

"Fakemon" is fan shorthand for original monster designs created outside the official franchise. Uranium's volunteer team spent years designing, spriting, and balancing those creatures so they would sit comfortably beside the classics, and community consensus is that they largely succeeded. What follows is a practical breakdown of the roster, the categories the game sorts those creatures into, and how to actually use them on a team.

## Why the Pokemon Uranium Fakemon Roster Stands Out

Plenty of fan games reuse existing sprites and tweak a few stats. Uranium took the harder road. A volunteer team worked on the project for roughly nine years, building an original region, an original soundtrack, and a Pokédex of original species that community counts typically place north of 150.

A few features separate it from the typical fan project:

- **An original region.** Tandor has its own towns, routes, gym leaders, and backstory rather than a redrawn Kanto or Johto.
- **A brand-new type.** The Nuclear type exists only in Uranium, and it reshapes how you build a team.
- **Complete evolution families.** Most fakemon arrive with two- or three-stage lines, so raising them feels like raising an official Pokémon.
- **Coexistence with the classics.** Familiar Pokémon still appear in the wild, which means your existing type knowledge stays useful.

### Design Philosophy in One Sentence

Uranium's creatures lean into the region's identity — industrial, irradiated, and a little darker than a mainline game — instead of copying the Kanto archetypes players already know by heart.

## Meet the Tandor Starters

Every journey in Uranium begins with one of three original starters, and each one fills a clearly defined role. The table below follows the community-maintained Uranium wiki; Orchynx and Metalynx are listed there as dex entries #001 and #002, both typed Grass/Steel.

| Dex # | First form | Type | Final form | Type | Team role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #001 | Orchynx | Grass / Steel | Metalynx | Grass / Steel | Defensive pivot with excellent resistances |
| #002 | Raptorch | Fire | Archilles | Fire / Ground | Fast physical wallbreaker |
| — | Eletux | Water | Electruxo | Water / Electric | Special attacker with strong coverage |

A quick note on each:

- **Orchynx** is the safest pick for newcomers. Grass/Steel is a rare defensive combination, and its Steel half blunts the Ice and Fairy attacks that normally punish Grass types.
- **Raptorch** rewards aggressive play. It hits hard and fast, but its Ground half leaves it exposed to Water and Ice moves, so you will need to switch intelligently.
- **Eletux** is the middle-of-the-road choice. Water/Electric coverage is genuinely awkward for most opponents to wall, and it scales well into the late game.

Player experience suggests that all three starters remain viable through the post-game, so pick the design you like rather than chasing a tier list.

## The Nuclear Type, Explained

The Nuclear type is Uranium's signature mechanic and the single biggest reason its fakemon feel distinct. It is tied directly to the region's radioactive backstory, and it produces some of the strangest risk-reward decisions in any Pokémon game.

| Question | Community-documented answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is it? | A radiation-themed type unique to Uranium |
| On offense | Nuclear moves deal super-effective damage to most types |
| On defense | Nuclear Pokémon are generally frail and take heavy hits in return |
| Signature users | Nucleon, Urayne |
| Availability | Mostly mid-to-late game and post-story |

The practical takeaway is that Nuclear is a glass cannon. A Nuclear attacker can punch through walls that would otherwise stall your team, but it usually cannot survive a second hit. Community reports also note that Steel types are the most common answer to Nuclear pressure, so keep a Fire or Fighting move in reserve.

Nucleon, an Eevee evolution that exists only in this game, is the most frequently recommended Nuclear option for a standard playthrough, while Urayne sits at the top of the legendary tier.

If you are a completionist, treat these categories as a checklist. Working through them in order keeps you from wandering Tandor aimlessly in the post-game.

## How to Build a Team Around Pokemon Uranium Fakemon

Team building in Uranium works much like it does in the official games, with one twist: you cannot rely on memorized matchups. Here is a role framework that community players recommend for a first playthrough.

| Team role | What to look for | Fakemon example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical wallbreaker | High Attack plus reliable STAB | Archilles line |
| Special attacker | High Sp. Atk and Speed | Electruxo line |
| Defensive pivot | Resistances to common attacking types | Metalynx |
| Nuclear nuke | Nuclear STAB for breaking stalls | Nucleon |
| Legendary anchor | Late-game catch with high base stats | Urayne |

Actionable tips that make a real difference:

1. **Always carry an answer to Steel.** Nuclear attacks are your best wall-breaking tool, and Steel is the type most likely to shrug them off.
2. **Do not build around more than one Nuclear Pokémon.** Their fragility means two on a team usually means two free knockouts for your opponent.
3. **Breed for natures early.** Because Uranium fakemon evolve through their own lines, investing in a good nature at level one saves hours of grinding later.
4. **Check evolution methods before training.** Several Uranium lines evolve under unusual conditions, and the community wiki is the most reliable reference.
5. **Rotate your sixth slot.** Uranium's routes introduce new species constantly, so treat that final slot as a tryout spot rather than a permanent fixture.

Following that framework, most players finish the main story with a core of four staples and two rotating specialists.

## FAQ

**How many Pokemon Uranium fakemon are there?**
Community counts commonly land north of 150 original species, and the wiki's numbering runs well past the starters at #001 and #002. Because the project was fan-made, exact totals depend on whether you count alternate forms and event-only creatures.

**Which Pokemon Uranium fakemon are the strongest?**
Player experience consistently points to the legendary tier — Urayne in particular — and to Nucleon as the most impactful non-legendary pick thanks to its Nuclear typing. That said, a well-trained starter can carry you through the main story without any legendary support.

**Is Pokemon Uranium still playable?**
Yes. The project's official download was pulled after legal requests from Nintendo, but the game remains widely discussed and the community wiki continues to document every species, type, and evolution method.

**What types are the Uranium starters?**
Orchynx and its evolution Metalynx are Grass/Steel, while the other two starters build toward Fire/Ground and Water/Electric final forms. That combination gives new players a clean triangle plus two secondary typings that stay relevant into the post-game.

Whether you are chasing a complete dex or just looking for a region where every route holds a surprise, the Pokemon Uranium fakemon deliver something the mainline games rarely can: genuine discovery.
