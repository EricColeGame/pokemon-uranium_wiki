Collecting badges is the heart of any Pokémon adventure, and the Pokemon Uranium gym leaders are the gatekeepers standing between you and the Tandor Championship. Because Uranium is a fan-made RPG with its own regional Pokédex — including the unusual Nuclear type — you can't simply coast on the habits you built in the mainline games. This guide covers how the Tandor League is structured, every documented badge and its obedience cap, and practical strategies for beating the Pokemon Uranium gym leaders without endless grinding.

## How the Tandor League Is Structured

The Tandor League is the region-wide challenge that trainers in Tandor take on, including your player character and your rival, Theo. The rules will feel familiar: travel the region, defeat the gym leaders spread across Tandor's cities, and collect eight badges. Once you hold all eight, you become eligible to enter the Tandor Championship and compete for the title of regional champion.

Each gym follows a theme — almost always a single Pokémon type — and each victory awards a badge that does two things: it proves your progress, and it raises the level at which traded Pokémon will obey you.

| Element | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Badges required for the Championship | Eight |
| Typical gym theme | One Pokémon type per leader |
| Where gyms are located | Cities across the Tandor region |
| Final challenge | The Tandor Championship |
| Ultimate reward | Regional Champion title |
| Rival progress | Theo advances alongside you |

That last detail matters more than most players expect. Badges in Uranium aren't just trophies — they're the mechanism that keeps a traded powerhouse from trivializing the early game.

## Every Tandor League Badge and Its Obedience Cap

Obedience is the single most misunderstood system in Uranium, and it's tied directly to the badges you earn from the Pokemon Uranium gym leaders. The rule is specific: with no badges, only Pokémon up to level 10 will obey you **if they have been traded**. Pokémon you caught yourself will always listen, which is why a self-caught team is the safest long-term investment.

Here is the badge progression documented in the community's [Tandor League badge and gym leader reference](https://pokemon-uranium.fandom.com/wiki/Tandor_League).

| Badge | Obedience cap for traded Pokémon |
| --- | --- |
| Normal Badge | Up to Lv. 20 |
| Bright Gem Badge | Up to Lv. 30 |
| Tide Badge | Up to Lv. 40 |
| Salsa Badge | Up to Lv. 60 |
| Drama Badge | Up to Lv. 70 |
| Apex Badge | Up to Lv. 80 |
| Zen Badge | All levels |

**A note on the gap:** the archived table jumps from the Tide Badge (Lv. 40) straight to the Salsa Badge (Lv. 60), which suggests a fourth badge with a Lv. 50 cap that the snapshot doesn't capture. Since the league requires eight badges total, treat this list as seven of eight and check the community wiki for the complete roster before you plan a challenge run.

### What the Obedience Rule Actually Means in Practice

- **Traded Pokémon are the only ones at risk.** A Lv. 45 traded mon will ignore your commands until you've earned the badge that raises the cap past its level.
- **Self-caught Pokémon never disobey.** If you're building a challenge team, catch it yourself.
- **The caps are a soft difficulty gate.** They discourage trading in a late-game ace to bulldoze the early gyms.
- **Eggs you hatch count as yours.** Hatching is a legitimate workaround for players who want strong early options.

## Gym Leader Spotlight: Maria of Nowtoch City

The first Pokemon Uranium gym leader most players meet is Maria, who runs the gym in Nowtoch City. She specializes in Normal-type Pokémon, and defeating her earns the Normal Badge — the key that raises your traded-Pokémon obedience cap to Lv. 20.

| Attribute | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Leader | Maria |
| Location | Nowtoch City Gym |
| Specialty | Normal-type Pokémon |
| Badge awarded | Normal Badge |
| Obedience cap after winning | Lv. 20 (traded Pokémon) |
| Best offensive answer | Fighting-type moves |
| Moves to avoid | Ghost-type attacks |

Normal is a deceptively simple type. It has exactly one weakness — Fighting — and it's completely immune to Ghost-type damage. Rock and Steel types also resist Normal attacks, so bulky Rock-types can wall Maria's team if you're short on Fighting coverage. Community reports consistently describe Maria as a fair but punishing first test for players who arrive underleveled or with a single-type team.

### A Simple Game Plan for Maria

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Level your core team to roughly Lv. 15–18 before challenging her |
| 2 | Lead with a Fighting-type, or any teammate carrying a Fighting move |
| 3 | Remove Ghost-type attacks from your movesets — they do nothing here |
| 4 | Bring two or three Potions and one status heal |
| 5 | Save a healthy Pokémon in the back for her final team member |

If you picked a starter that lacks Fighting coverage, a simple low-level Fighting-type caught on an earlier route is usually enough. You don't need to over-invest — you need the right type matchup.

## Preparing for the Rest of the Pokemon Uranium Gym Leaders

Maria is the tutorial; the later gyms are the exam. Tandor's gym leaders each lean into a different type, and the region's custom Pokédex means you'll face creatures whose typings you can't predict from memory. That's why type coverage beats raw power for most of the run.

| Fighting | Normal, Rock, Steel, Ice, Dark | Psychic, Flying, Fairy |
| Fire | Grass, Bug, Ice, Steel | Water, Rock, Ground |
| Water | Fire, Rock, Ground | Grass, Electric |
| Electric | Water, Flying | Ground (full immunity) |
| Ground | Electric, Fire, Rock, Poison, Steel | Water, Grass, Flying |
| Grass | Water, Ground, Rock | Fire, Flying, Bug, Ice, Poison |

### Build a Team, Not a Carry

The most common failure state among players chasing the Pokemon Uranium gym leaders is a single overleveled starter supported by five neglected teammates. Once you hit a gym that resists your starter's type, the run stalls hard.

- **Carry six trained Pokémon.** Rotate them so nobody falls more than a few levels behind.
- **Keep at least two attacking types per Pokémon where possible.** Coverage moves win gym battles.
- **Pack a dedicated wall.** A high-defense Pokémon that can absorb a hit buys you free turns.
- **Stock status moves.** Paralysis and burn are often more valuable than a third attack.
- **Respect the Nuclear type.** Tandor's signature typing hits unusually hard and has very few safe switch-ins. Treat any Nuclear opponent as a boss fight and plan your pivot carefully.

### A Gym-Ready Checklist

Before you walk through any gym door, run this list:

1. Every team member is within two levels of the gym's expected range.
2. You have a super-effective attacking option for the leader's type.
3. Your bag holds at least five healing items.
4. No traded Pokémon on your team exceeds the current obedience cap.
5. You've saved recently — gym losses cost time, not progress, but replanning is easier with a fresh save.

## Common Mistakes That Cost You Badges

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trading in a high-level Pokémon early | It won't obey you until the cap rises | Train self-caught Pokémon until late game |
| Ignoring the badge table | You may over-level a traded mon that then ignores you | Check the obedience cap after each badge |
| Running one attack per Pokémon | You get walled by a single resist | Teach coverage moves via TMs |
| Skipping optional routes | You miss useful catches and items | Explore before every gym |
| Assuming the mainline type chart applies everywhere | Uranium's custom types break expectations | Test unfamiliar typings in low-stakes battles |

None of these are fatal, but each one adds hours of grinding. Fixing them early makes the mid-game dramatically smoother.

## FAQ

**How many gym leaders are in Pokemon Uranium?**
The Tandor League requires eight badges, so you'll face eight gym leaders before you're allowed into the Tandor Championship. Community references document the badge order, though some archived lists are incomplete, so verify the full roster on the wiki if you're planning a challenge run.

**Who is the first gym leader you face?**
Maria, the Normal-type leader in Nowtoch City. She awards the Normal Badge, which raises the obedience cap for traded Pokémon to Lv. 20.

**Do badges make my Pokémon obey me in Pokemon Uranium?**
Yes — but only for traded Pokémon. Each badge raises the level cap at which a traded Pokémon will follow your commands, starting at Lv. 10 with no badges and ending with the final badge, which removes the restriction entirely. Pokémon you caught or hatched yourself always obey.

**What happens after you beat all the Pokemon Uranium gym leaders?**
Once you hold all eight badges, you qualify for the Tandor Championship, where you compete for the title of regional champion. Expect the Championship battles to be a significant step up from the gyms — bring a fully leveled, type-diverse team and plenty of healing items.

The Tandor League rewards preparation over raw grinding. Learn the badge caps, build real type coverage, and treat every gym as a puzzle to solve rather than a wall to smash through — and the Pokemon Uranium gym leaders become some of the most satisfying battles in the game.
