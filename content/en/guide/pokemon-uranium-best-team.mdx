## Why Team Building in Pokemon Uranium Is Different

Pokemon Uranium is a fan-made monster-catching RPG with an original regional dex, a full gym circuit, and one mechanic that rewrites everything you know about type matchups: the Nuclear type. If you have been hunting for the Pokemon Uranium best team, the short answer is that you need broad coverage, one dependable tank, and a hard answer to Nuclear-types. The long answer is the six-slot blueprint below.

Assembling the Pokemon Uranium best team is not about stacking the six strongest creatures in the dex. It is about covering the matchups this game actually throws at you — the gym leaders, the Victory Road gauntlet, and a championship bracket that recycles those same leaders with better AI and higher levels.

Here is the rule that changes everything: Nuclear-type attacks hit almost everything for super effective damage, and Nuclear-type Pokémon take super effective damage from almost everything in return. That is why a single Nuclear-type can carve through the entire championship while fainting to a stiff breeze. Speed, priority moves, and safe switch-ins matter more than raw stats.

Two other systems shape your roster. Mega Evolution gives several key picks a second gear, and the level curve climbs steeply after the seventh badge. Before you commit a rare TM, it is worth verifying move lists and type charts on the [official Pokemon Uranium website](https://pokemonuranium.org/), since forum threads and fan wikis sometimes disagree on the details.

## The Pokemon Uranium Best Team: Six Core Picks

Community reports and player experience converge on a remarkably consistent core of six. The table below shows the lineup, the role each member fills, and why it earns a permanent slot.

| Slot | Pokemon | Typing | Role | Why it earns a slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Metalynx | Grass / Steel | Physical tank, Mega candidate | Resists most gyms, fears only Fire; learns Night Slash to punish Rosalind |
| 2 | Dunseraph | Normal / Dragon | Bulky special attacker | Evolves from Dunsparce at level 42; huge movepool, but 4x weak to Nuclear |
| 3 | Terlard | Ground / Dragon | Fast physical sweeper | Strong base stats from level 22 onward; shreds Fire and Steel gyms |
| 4 | Nucleon | Nuclear | Special sweeper | Hits nearly everything super effectively and resists Nuclear in return |
| 5 | Pajay | Fire / Flying | Special attacker | Hits hard from its pre-evolution; carries a huge chunk of the late game |
| 6 | S51-A | Psychic / Steel | Bulky special attacker | Gifted to you around level 40; the safest switch-in for the final boss |

Metalynx is the most frequently recommended starter in community threads, and the reasoning is simple: Grass/Steel resists a shocking number of gym types, it is neutral against Nuclear rather than weak to it, and its learnset is deep enough to run physical sets without help from TMs. Mega Evolution pushes it from "solid" to "carries the mid game."

Dunseraph is the patience pick. Dunsparce is genuinely mediocre before level 42, so if you raise one, plan on babysitting it through the early badges. After it evolves, you get a bulky attacker with Dragon Pulse, Air Slash, Flamethrower, and Roost — a combination that beats a lot of endgame threats on coverage alone.

Nucleon is the engine. Its ability turns Normal-type moves into Nuclear-type moves, which means Hyper Voice becomes one of the most efficient attacks in the entire game. Just remember the trade-off: it is frail, it is weak to basically everything, and it should never be your lead.

## Recommended Movesets and Roles

Movesets matter more here than in most fan games because coverage gaps get punished hard in the championship. These sets are drawn from player experience and community discussions.

| Pokemon | Recommended moves | Role notes |
|---|---|---|
| Metalynx | Leaf Blade, Iron Tail, Earthquake, + one Grass or utility slot | Physical tank; Mega Evolve when you need to absorb a big hit |
| Dunseraph | Dragon Pulse, Air Slash, Flamethrower, Roost | Bulky special attacker with reliable recovery |
| Terlard | Earthquake, Dragon Rush, Stone Edge, Iron Tail | Fast physical coverage; switch it in on Fire and Steel |
| Nucleon | Hyper Voice, Shadow Ball, Quick Attack, + coverage | Ability converts Normal moves to Nuclear; Hyper Voice is the payoff |
| Pajay | Flamethrower, Air Slash, Will-O-Wisp, U-turn | Special attacker; U-turn out of anything faster |
| S51-A | Psychic, Energy Ball, Focus Blast, Flash Cannon | Slow but sturdy; your designated pivot for the champion's ace |

The math behind Nucleon is worth spelling out. Community calculations put Hyper Voice at roughly 180 to 360 effective base power depending on whether the target has one type or two, because Nuclear is super effective against nearly every type in the game. Steel and Nuclear are the only reliable resists.

For EV spreads, keep it simple:

- **Nucleon and Pajay:** Special Attack and Speed
- **Metalynx and S51-A:** HP and Defense (or HP and Special Defense)
- **Terlard:** Attack and Speed
- **Dunseraph:** HP and Special Attack

Leveling is the other half of the equation. Community reports suggest the endgame curve sits near level 70, with the toughest trainers pushing toward 80, so arriving underleveled is a losing strategy.

| Stage | Level target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Early gyms (1–3) | 18–25 | Terlard and Metalynx carry these fights |
| Mid game (4–6) | 35–45 | Dunsparce evolves at 42 — this is the payoff window |
| Late gyms / Victory Road | 55–65 | Start EV training and finalize movesets here |
| Championship | 65–70+ | Community reports the curve near 70, with the highest trainers around 80 |

A few practical habits make the endgame much less painful:

- Rotate all six members so nobody falls behind the curve
- Keep a full stock of healing items before the championship bracket
- Save immediately before the final boss — its ace hits hard enough to end a run
- Use your Mega Evolution on the Pokémon that patches your weakest matchup, not automatically on your favorite

## FAQ

**What is the best starter for a Pokemon Uranium best team run?**
Metalynx, the Grass/Steel starter, is the most commonly recommended choice because of its defensive typing, deep learnset, and Mega Evolution. That said, any starter works if you build coverage around its weaknesses — the other five slots matter more than slot one.

**Do I need Nucleon on my team?**
No, but community reports describe it as a "Hail Mary" pick that can one-shot most of the championship. If you are stuck, it is the fastest fix in the game. If you want a real challenge, leave it in the box.

**What level should my team be before the championship?**
Aim for at least 65, and ideally closer to 70. Community reports place the normal endgame curve around level 70, with the strongest trainers near 80. EV training at that point matters as much as the levels themselves.

**How do I evolve Dunsparce into Dunseraph?**
Player experience says Dunsparce evolves at level 42. Before then it is a weak link, so keep it in the back of the party and let Metalynx or Terlard handle the early gyms while it catches up.
